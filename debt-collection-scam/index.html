What a fake debt collection letter often looks like

A letter, email or text from a “collection agency” says you owe money: for an order, a subscription or a contract you can't place. It adds collection fees and threatens court, a credit record entry or a visit. Common signs: no clear original creditor or invoice number, a debt you can't match to anything, fees larger than the debt, a very short deadline, payment only by transfer to an unfamiliar account, gift cards or a link, and threats meant to scare you into paying today. Real collection agencies exist too, so check rather than guess.

What to do next

Don't pay, call the number on the letter, click links or open attachments until you have checked. Look for the original invoice, order or contract in your own records. Contact the original company using the details from your own records or its official website, not the ones in the letter, and ask whether anything is open and whether they passed it to a collection agency.

Still unsure? Ask CalmClick a follow-up question or show the explanation to someone you trust. Checking should be quick.

A real collection letter can have a deadline, and an official notice from a court or debt enforcement office (such as a payment order; in Switzerland from the Betreibungsamt) often has a very short one, sometimes only days, shown on the document itself. Don't ignore it or leave it unopened. If it is real and correct, act by the date given. If you think it is wrong, an objection to an official notice must go to the court or office named in it, not only to the collection agency, so act at once. If you can't tell and a deadline is close, contact a consumer or debt advice service or someone you trust right away.

If you already paid

Stay calm: this happens to many careful people. Call your bank using the number on your card or your bank's official website and tell them it may be a scam payment; the sooner, the better, though a payment cannot always be stopped. You can also report it to the police. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.
