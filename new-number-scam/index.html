What the message usually looks like

A text or messenger message from an unknown number says: “Hi Mum, my phone broke, this is my new number.” Or “Hi Dad”. After a short chat comes a request: an urgent bill, a payment that “has to go out today”, often with an excuse why they can't call right now. Common signs: a new number, a familiar tone, urgency, and a request for money or a code.

What to do next

Don't send money and don't share any code. Call your child or relative on the number you already have saved, or ask another family member. If you can't reach them, ask something only they would know, or ask another family member, and still send nothing. A real family member will understand that you check first. You can block and report the unknown number in your messaging app. Still unsure? Ask CalmClick a follow-up question or show the explanation to someone you trust.

If you already paid

Stay calm: this happens to many careful, loving parents. Call your bank right away using the number on your card or your bank's official website; tell them it was a scam payment; the sooner, the better, but stopping a transfer is not always possible. You can also report it to the police. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.
