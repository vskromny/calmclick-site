What a fake prize message often looks like

A text, email, letter or call says you have won: a lottery, a prize draw, a gift card, a phone or a holiday. To collect it, you are asked to pay a small fee, “tax” or delivery charge, give your bank or card details, send ID, buy gift cards, or call an expensive number. Common signs: a prize from a draw you don't remember entering, a fee before you can receive anything, a short deadline, a request to keep it secret, and contact details you can't check.

What to do next

Don't pay anything, don't share bank, card or ID details, and don't call numbers or click links in the message. A genuine prize is not normally released only after you pay a fee or buy gift cards first. If you did enter a competition, look up the organiser yourself through its official website and ask them directly. Still unsure? Ask CalmClick a follow-up question or show the explanation to someone you trust. There is no need to rush: a deadline in the message is not a reason to act before you have checked.

If you already paid or shared details

Stay calm: this happens to many careful people. If you paid or gave card or bank details, call your bank using the number on your card or your bank's official website and tell them it may be a scam; the sooner, the better, though a payment cannot always be stopped. If you sent a copy of your ID, keep a note of it and watch for unexpected letters or accounts. You can also report it to the police. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.
