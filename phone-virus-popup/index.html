What a fake virus warning usually looks like

A pop-up or a full-screen page says your phone is infected, hacked or at risk, often with a countdown, a loud tone or a warning sign. It asks you to tap a button, install an app, call a number or pay to “fix” it. Be careful with any web page that pushes you to install something or call a number to remove a virus. Common signs: alarm and urgency, a timer, and a page you can't easily close.

What to do next

Don't tap the buttons, don't call the number and don't install anything from the pop-up. Close the browser tab; if it won't close, close the browser app. Optional: in Settings, you can clear your browser's history and website data. This signs you out of websites, so only do it if you are comfortable signing in again. Still unsure? Ask CalmClick a follow-up question or send the explanation to someone you trust.

If you already called or paid

Stay calm: this happens to many careful people. If you paid or shared card details, call your bank using the number on your card or your bank's official website. If someone got remote access or a password, change that password. If you installed an app from the pop-up, delete it, and ask someone you trust for help if you are unsure. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.
