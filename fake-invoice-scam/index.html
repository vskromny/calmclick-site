What a fake invoice often looks like

An email, letter or PDF asks you to pay a bill you don't recognise: an order you never placed, a renewal you didn't expect, a “final reminder” or a fee with a deadline. Sometimes it looks like a company you know. Common signs: a payment you can't match to anything, pressure to pay fast, new bank details, a link or attachment to “view the invoice”, and threats of extra fees.

What to do next

Don't pay, click links or open attachments until you have checked. Look for the order or contract in your own records. Contact the company using the details from your own records or its official website, not the ones on the invoice. Still unsure? Ask CalmClick a follow-up question or show the explanation to someone you trust. Checking should be quick. If it turns out to be a real bill, pay it by the due date, because a genuine invoice or reminder that goes unpaid can bring late fees or collection steps. If you can't tell and a deadline is close, contact the company using your own records and ask for more time.

If you already paid

Stay calm: this happens to many careful people. Call your bank using the number on your card or your bank's official website and tell them it may be a scam payment; the sooner, the better, though a payment cannot always be stopped. You can also report it to the police. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.
