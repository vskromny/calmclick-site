What a fake delivery text usually looks like

It says a parcel is held, a delivery failed, or a small fee is due, and asks you to tap a link to “reschedule” or pay. Be careful with any text that asks you to pay through a link. If you are expecting a parcel, check with the courier directly. Common signs: a short or odd link, a deadline like “today only”, and a sender you don't recognise.

Take a photo, get a plain answer

Take a photo or a screenshot of the text with CalmClick. It reads the message for you and explains in plain words what it seems to be, with a three-level read: “No clear warning signs”, “Caution”, or “Warning”, each with an icon and a word. When it is unsure, it says so and tries to lean toward caution.

What to do next

Don't tap the link and don't enter card details. If you are expecting a parcel, check its status on the courier's own website or app, typed in yourself, not from the text. Still unsure? Ask CalmClick a follow-up question or send the explanation to someone you trust.

If you already tapped or paid

Stay calm: this happens to many careful people. If you entered card details, call your bank using the number on your card. Change any password you typed in. Your bank can tell you what to do next. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.
